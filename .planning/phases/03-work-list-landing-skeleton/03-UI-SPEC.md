## The type budget: no fifth size, and why one was considered

The inherited contract is **exactly four sizes and two weights**. Phase 2 wanted a fifth (a 24px "Deck"), was blocked, and rebuilt its hierarchy from variants. Phase 3 **adds no type role, no size, no weight, and no face.** Every string on every surface below is one of the four shipped roles.

There is exactly one place a fifth size was tempting, and it is named here so the rejection is on the record rather than implicit:

**The work-list entry titles.** The featured slot takes the `Heading` role (Humane, `clamp(2rem, 1rem + 4vw, 4.5rem)`). If work-list titles also took `Heading` they would render at **the identical size** — 32px at 375px, 72px at 1440px — and the featured slot would have no primacy at all, which fails `HOME-02` before Phase 4 starts. The next role down is `Body` at 18px. A 24px step between them would have been the conventional answer.

**Rejected.** The differentiator this system already owns is *face*, not size: the featured headline is Humane (ultra-condensed, poster) and the work titles are Newsreader (text serif). Face plus a 14–54px size gap is a far larger perceptual jump than 24px-vs-18px inside one face would have been. A fifth size would have bought a smaller distinction than the one already available for free. The budget stands at four.

## Color

Inherited palette, unchanged. `app/globals.css` `@theme` already defines every value below.

| Role | Value | Usage |
|------|-------|-------|
| Dominant (60%) | `#ffffff` (`--color-paper`) | Page background and every surface on `/` and `/cv`. **No section on any Phase 3 surface has a different background.** |
| Secondary (30%) | `#000000` (`--color-ink`) | All text — nameplate, positioning sentence, contents links at rest, section heads, featured headline and copy, work titles, annotations, ordinals, host labels, stub copy, back links. Also the 1px full-ink rule beneath every section head. |
| Accent (10%) | `#C1272D` (`--color-accent`) | **Reserved for exactly two things, unchanged from Phases 1 and 2:** (1) the `:focus-visible` outline on any link, (2) link `:hover` / `:focus` text colour and underline colour. Nothing else. |
| Destructive | not applicable | No destructive action exists on any Phase 3 surface: no forms, no mutations, no visitor-changeable state. |

**Accent reserved for:** focus-visible outlines and link hover/focus states. That is the complete list, and this phase does not widen it. Specifically — the accent is **not** used for: the contents list at rest, the ordinals `01`/`02`, any section head or its rule, the featured slot or its kicker, the work-list host labels, any stub, or the heading trail (which carries its own scroll-driven `hsl()` cycle, Phase 1).

**On the trail's hue cycle, and why it is not a precedent.** The trail is a second chromatic system on this site: `HUE_SPEED = 110`, start hue `345`, advanced on scroll samples and frozen when the page is still. It sits outside the 60/30/10 split and outside the accent reservation. That exclusion is **specific to the inherited trail** — a single, already-shipped, user-validated decorative treatment, bounded to the shadow layers behind Humane glyphs whose own ink never changes (Phase 1, Motion Contract). **It is not permission to introduce a third chromatic system.** Any later phase that wants colour beyond ink, paper, accent and the trail's own layers — a tinted section, a coloured rule, a hue on a stub, a second decorative cycle — is reopening the palette and must say so explicitly rather than citing the trail as precedent. The one bounded exemption already granted elsewhere is Shiki's token palette inside `<pre>`, which Phase 2 justified on chart-data-palette footing; that list is closed at two.

### Contrast, measured against the actual background

Phase 2 shipped a wrong Shiki theme because a ratio was computed against `#ffffff` while the element sat on a `#F5F5F5` tint. The guard against repeating that is stated as a fact about this phase, not as an intention:

**Phase 3 introduces no tinted surface, no filled block, and no new colour token.** Every glyph on `/` and `/cv` sits on `#ffffff` directly. Therefore:

| Foreground | Background (actual) | Ratio | Floor | Verdict |
|------------|---------------------|-------|-------|---------|
| `#000000` ink text, all roles | `#ffffff` | 21:1 | 4.5:1 | pass |
| `#C1272D` accent, link hover/focus text | `#ffffff` | **5.84:1** | 4.5:1 (text) | pass |
| `#C1272D` accent, focus-visible outline | `#ffffff` | **5.84:1** | 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11) | pass |
| `#000000` 1px section-head rule | `#ffffff` | 21:1 | n/a (non-informational) | pass |
| `rgba(0,0,0,0.12)` `--color-rule` row separator | `#ffffff` → `#E0E0E0` | 1.3:1 | n/a (non-informational separator, carries no information not also carried by spacing and structure) | pass |

**Standing instruction carried forward:** if any later phase introduces a tint or fill behind text on these surfaces, every ratio in this table must be **re-measured against the composited colour**, not against `#ffffff`. The `--color-surface-code` tint resolves to `#F5F5F5`; that number exists in this repo and is the precedent for how easily this goes wrong.

### Aesthetic guardrails (BRIEF §8 / anti-goal #3 — "looks like data, isn't")

The landing introduces the site's first ordered list and its first non-prose rules, so the trap gets closer. The checkable rule stands: *does this element take a data prop, or is every coordinate hardcoded — and does it read as axis, tick, or plotted point?*

- **The `01` / `02` ordinals are list markers on a real `<ol>` of real entries.** They take their value from the entry's position in `lib/work.ts`. Order is editorial and is the only thing they encode. They must never gain: a rule connecting them, a fixed-interval repetition independent of item count, a scale, or a value other than the item's index.
- **The rules on this page are typographic separators bound to content** — a section head's underline and a between-rows hairline. They must never gain tick marks, terminal serifs, vertical companions, or numeric labels.
- **Phase 1's optional ornamental rule grid (a 1px rule every `12.5vh`) stays unshipped, and Phase 3 explicitly declines it.** A page that already carries a numbered list and horizontal separators, overlaid with a background rule grid at fixed viewport intervals, is precisely the dangerous middle BRIEF §8 describes: geometry at regular intervals that encodes nothing, in front of people trained to read gridlines. Declined on the record so a later phase does not add it casually.
- **No decorative element of any kind ships in this phase.** No wedge, no diagonal, no generative background, no photograph (`PROF-02` is Phase 6).
- **No rounded corners anywhere.** Nothing on these surfaces has a fill or a border box to round.
- **No full-viewport first screen.** `min-h-screen` is removed from `/` (Amendment A1). The site is content-led, not hero-led (`PROJECT.md`, BRIEF §9 anti-goal #2); the featured section must be reachable by a short scroll at 1440px, not gated behind a viewport-height nameplate.

### The heading trail on the landing view `[DISCRETION]`

CONTEXT delegated *"how the heading trail applies to landing section headings"* to this stage — Phase 1 left trail scope open and this is the first phase with real section headings. Resolved:

| Element | Face | Trail |
|---------|------|-------|
| `/` nameplate `<h1>` (Display) | Humane | **Yes** |
| Featured headline `<h3>` (Heading) | Humane | **Yes** |
| `/cv` `<h1>` (Heading) | Humane | **Yes** |
| Landing section heads — *Case study / Work / Backlog / Contact* (Label) | Newsreader | **No** |
| Work-list entry titles (Standfirst) | Newsreader | **No** |
| Contents links, annotations, host labels, stub copy, back links | Newsreader | **No** |

The rule is unchanged and unambiguous: **Humane gets the trail; Newsreader never does.** A 240-layer stacked shadow on a 14px uppercase section head would be visual noise at reading scale and re-opens the contrast risk for nothing.

**The landing therefore registers exactly two trail-carrying headings, and `/cv` registers one.** For calibration: the shipped `/type` specimen already registers five and is live, so this is conservative rather than novel.

### Two properties of the shipped smear system that constrain this phase

Read from `components/smear-heading/smear-heading-provider.tsx` and `use-smear-heading.ts`. Neither is a bug; both are constraints the planner must design around.

1. **Cost is linear in *registered* headings, not in *visible* ones.** `frame()` iterates the whole registry every animation frame and calls `draw()` for each entry regardless of viewport position. An off-screen heading whose `lagY` is clamped at `MAX_TRAIL` still writes up to 240 `text-shadow` layers per frame. Capping the landing at two trail headings is what keeps this bounded. **If a later phase adds trail-carrying headings to a long page, the correct fix is to skip the `draw()` DOM write when the element's rect lies outside the viewport by more than `MAX_TRAIL` — and nothing else.** That is an additive guard, not a retune: the `anyActive` bookkeeping must stay driven by `distance > 0.15` so the loop still settles, and no constant may change. `planner may override` on whether to add the guard now; it is not required at two headings.

2. **`documentTop` is captured once, after `document.fonts.ready`, and never recomputed.** Any post-mount layout change *above* a registered heading leaves its trail anchored to a stale document offset, and the heading will smear from the wrong origin. The landing has no images, no lazy content and no client-side layout in Phase 3, so this is safe today. **Forward note for Phase 6 (`PROF-02`):** the photograph of Guillem lands on a surface above trail-carrying headings. It must reserve its space with explicit intrinsic dimensions or an `aspect-ratio`, or the trail below it desyncs. This is `BUILD-06`'s no-layout-shift posture and a trail-correctness requirement at the same time.

### Everything else that moves

- **The link colour transition: 120ms `color` (and `text-decoration-color` where a rest underline exists), `ease-out`.** Identical to the shipped `.prose-site a` rule. Nothing else on any Phase 3 surface animates — no fade-in on scroll, no stagger on the work list, no page transitions, no hover elevation.
- **No smooth scrolling.** `scroll-behavior: smooth` must **not** be set for the contents list's in-page anchors. Two reasons, both concrete: it is motion, and gating it correctly means another `prefers-reduced-motion` branch for no gain; and a smooth scroll fires a long burst of `scroll` events, each of which advances `trailHue` in `handleScroll()` — a single anchor click would spin the trail's hue through a large arc, which is a visible artefact rather than a considered effect. Anchor jumps are instant.
- **Standing rule, restated because it is easy to drop:** every hover/focus transition must be wrapped in `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)` at the moment it is written, not retrofitted. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` a link still changes to accent and still gains its hover underline — the *state change* is not motion and must be preserved; only the transition duration is removed.

### The contents list `[DISCRETION]`

CONTEXT delegated *"navigation form — persistent header, in-page anchors, an index block, or a combination."* Resolved: **an in-flow contents list inside the page header. Not a persistent header, not sticky, not fixed, not repeated in a footer.**

- **Order:** `Work · Writing · Backlog · CV · Contact`. Reader-importance order, not page order — `Writing` sits second because it is the only route in the list that already holds shipped content.
- **Targets:** `Work` → `#work`, `Writing` → `/writing`, `Backlog` → `#backlog`, `CV` → `/cv`, `Contact` → `#contact`.
- **No separator glyph.** A 24px horizontal gap and 16px row gap separate the items; a middot or pipe between uppercase Label items is ornament the spacing already provides. (`PostMeta`'s shipped `·` stays as-is — that is a run of text inside one line, a different problem.)
- **Anchors and routes are not visually distinguished from each other.** Both keep the visitor on the site; a marker would be noise. Outbound links are a different case and *are* marked — see below.
- **Not sticky, and this is deliberate.** A fixed element on a page whose entire visual idea is a scroll-driven trail competes with that trail, and it introduces a viewport-anchored element the smear driver has no knowledge of. The page is five sections; scrolling back is the return path.
- **No skip link.** A skip link exists to bypass repeated chrome. There is no repeated chrome — the nav appears once, holds five links, and sits after a single `<h1>` and one sentence.

### Featured slot contract (`CASE-03`, `D-07`, `D-10`) `[DISCRETION]`

CONTEXT delegated *"whether the featured slot is visually distinct from the work list or is the work list's first entry given primacy."* **Resolved: a distinct section above the work list, not the work list's first row.** Three reasons:

1. **They point at different things (`D-07`).** The featured slot links inward to the case study at `/writing/{slug}`; work-list entries link outward to independently-hosted live pieces. 3. **The same project would otherwise appear twice in one list.** The case study is *about* the Mallorca piece, which is also work-list entry `01`. As two separate sections — "Case study" and "Work" — the asymmetry `D-07` describes is legible. As one list it reads as duplication or padding.

## Link and section-head contract

Three new CSS classes in `app/globals.css`. **Every declaration is a value already shipped in that file.** No new colour, no new size, no new duration, no new easing, no new offset.

### `.section-head`

The landing's section marker. Identical in every visual respect to the shipped `.prose-site h2` — Label role plus a 1px full-ink rule — minus the prose margins, because on the landing the surrounding flex `gap` owns the spacing.

```
font: Label role (14px / 400 / 1.3, uppercase, letter-spacing 0.04em, Newsreader)
padding-bottom: 8px        (sm)
border-bottom: 1px solid var(--color-ink)
```

This is the "1px full ink = structural" rule from Phase 2's three-weight budget. Phase 3 introduces **no fourth rule weight**: it uses 1px full ink (structural) and 1px `--color-rule` (separator) and nothing else.

### `.link` — links inside a block of body copy, outside `.prose-site`

Values copied verbatim from `.prose-site a`.

```
rest:           color inherit; underline, 1px thickness, text-underline-offset 0.12em,
                decoration colour currentColor
hover / focus:  color and decoration colour → var(--color-accent)
focus-visible:  outline 2px solid var(--color-accent); outline-offset 2px
transition:     120ms color + text-decoration-color, ease-out,
                inside @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)
```

Used where a link sits inside running prose outside the prose layer. **No Phase 3 surface currently needs it** — the class ships so that the next phase to write a linked sentence outside `.prose-site` (Phase 5's backlog descriptions, Phase 6's contact block) has a settled answer rather than inventing one. It is demonstrated on `/type` (A4).

### `.link-quiet` — standalone links that are not inside a block of text

Headlines-as-links, list-entry titles, navigation items, back links, the language switch.

```
rest:           color inherit; no underline
hover / focus:  color → var(--color-accent), plus an underline at 1px thickness
                and text-underline-offset 0.12em in var(--color-accent)
focus-visible:  outline 2px solid var(--color-accent); outline-offset 2px
transition:     120ms color, ease-out,
                inside @media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)
```

**Why no rest underline is allowed here.** WCAG 1.4.1 requires a non-colour distinction for links *within a block of text*, so that colour-blind readers can find them in a paragraph. None of `.link-quiet`'s uses is inside a block of text: they are a poster-scale headline, an `<h3>` list-entry title, items in a `<nav>` list, and a back link on its own line. Position and role identify them. A 1px underline beneath a 72px Humane headline, or beneath every one of five uppercase nav items, is heavy enough to change the design without adding information. **The hover state adds the underline back**, so the hover affordance is not colour-only either.

**Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, both classes keep the full colour and underline change** and lose only the transition. The state change is not motion.

**No other link styling may be introduced.** Two classes plus `.prose-site a` is the complete set for the site. If a later phase needs a third, it re-opens this section.

## Accessibility contract

Stated explicitly because this is the first phase with real navigation and multiple links per screen.

- **Focus visibility.** Every link on every Phase 3 surface has `:focus-visible { outline: 2px solid var(--color-accent); outline-offset: 2px }`. `outline: none` must not appear anywhere. Accent on paper is 5.84:1, past the 3:1 floor for non-text UI (WCAG 1.4.11).
- **Target size (WCAG 2.5.8, AA).** The floor is 24×24 CSS px, or ≥24px centre-to-centre spacing between undersized targets.
  - Label-role links **on their own line** — the five contents links, both site-root back links, both `← Writing` / `← Texte` back links — render an 18.2px line box, under the floor. They take `display: inline-block; padding-block: 4px` (`xs`) → 26.2px. On-grid, no exception needed.
  - The contents list's gaps are 24px horizontal (`lg`) and 16px vertical (`md`); with a 26.2px row height, vertical centre-to-centre is 42px. Clears the spacing route as well.
  - Work-list title links render a 27px line box (18px × 1.5) — clears the floor with no padding.
  - The featured headline clears it by a wide margin.
  - **`LanguageSwitch` is exempt and takes no padding.** It sits inline inside `PostMeta`'s text line, which is WCAG 2.5.8's explicit inline exception. Adding `inline-block` padding there would also change the shipped meta line's height on `/writing` and every post — a visual regression for no accessibility gain.
- **Landmarks and headings.** One `<main>` per page. `<nav aria-label="Sections">`. Each landing section is `<section aria-labelledby="{id}-head">`. The heading outline is a single `<h1>` (the nameplate), four `<h2>` section heads, and `<h3>` for the featured headline and each work-list title. No level is skipped and no heading is used for its size alone.
- **List semantics.** `<ol role="list">` for the work list, because `list-style: none` drops list semantics in Safari. The visible `01`/`02` ordinals are `aria-hidden="true"`.
- **Anchor navigation.** Instant, no smooth scroll. `scroll-margin-top: 32px` on each `section[id]` so the head is not flush to the viewport edge after a jump.
- **Reduced motion.** The trail's `start()` early-return already produces fully static headings. Link transitions are wrapped in `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`. There is nothing else that moves.
- **Contrast.** Every ratio in the Color section is measured against `#ffffff`, which is the actual composited background of every Phase 3 element — no tinted surface exists on these routes.

This is the direct cost of keeping the featured slot's primacy inside a four-size budget, and it is the right trade — a two-row list where row one is 72px and row two is 18px reads as a broken list, not a hierarchy. **If the optical pass finds the work section reads thin at 1440px, the remedy is more space and the existing rules, not a fifth type size** — the same remedy Phase 2 designated for its `h2`/`h3` signal.

2. **The landing announces an unwritten piece for the duration of Phase 3.** The featured slot's interim state puts "The case study is being written." at Heading scale in the first content section. Honest in-progress framing is correct *here* (`BRIEF` §5 principle 5) — an empty section saying so is candour, a whole site saying so is an excuse — and the window is short and noindexed. It is nonetheless covered by the launch gate above: this must never be the public launch condition.

**Designated optical checkpoint:** `/` and `/cv` at **375px and 1440px**. The 375px pass is the one that catches real failures — whether the five-item contents list wraps to a readable two or three rows rather than a ragged column, whether the work-list ordinal-above-title stack reads as one row rather than four loose lines, and whether the nameplate at its 56px floor still sits comfortably above the positioning sentence. The 1440px pass checks the opposite risk: whether a 72px Humane featured headline and an 18px work list on one screen read as hierarchy rather than as two unrelated pages.
